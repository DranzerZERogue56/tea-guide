## Not verified (left unchanged)

Caffeine ranges and brew details for most individual teas were only checked against type-level sources (black, green, white, oolong), not per tea. Individual teas whose specific facts could not be confirmed from a fetched page:
- hojicha caffeine (7-15 mg), kukicha caffeine (5-15 mg), awa-bancha caffeine (5-10 mg), purple-tea caffeine (10-20 mg): no page gave a per-tea figure.
- tamaryokucha origin detail (Ureshino, Saga): Wikipedia says only Kyushu.
- junshan-yinzhen "tribute tea for emperors", moonlight-white drying details, dong-ding and jin-xuan regions, turkish-black Rize, nilgiri "Blue Mountains of Tamil Nadu", assam Brahmaputra valley, kabusecha 2 week shading: plausible but not confirmed on a fetched page.
- gaba-oolong processing and origin (Wikipedia GABA tea page returned 404): only the health claims were removed.
- Brew amounts and times for most Chinese and Taiwanese teas (gongfu ratios): left as they were.
- All flavoured blends (mango, peach, strawberry, etc.): caffeine figures follow the base tea type and were not checked per product.
- The raspberry-leaf pregnancy statement could not be sourced, so it was removed.

## Source conflicts for a human to decide

- Matcha caffeine and temperature: the site says High (70 mg/cup) and 75-80 C. WK says 60-70 mg per 2 g serving, DAVIDsTEA says 20-40 mg per 8 oz, Wikipedia gives brewing water of 80 C (koicha) and 90 C (usucha). Left unchanged.
- Assam and English Breakfast say 50-90 mg/cup, while WK gives black tea as 40-70 mg (USDA average about 47 mg). Left unchanged.
- Pu-erh: DAVIDsTEA 14-125 mg per cup, so both the High (puerh-sheng) and Medium (puerh) labels are plausible.
- Yerba mate is in the high group but WK gives 30-85 mg, which overlaps the medium range. The label was corrected, the bucket left as high.
- The site buckets are loose compared with merchant ratings (DAVIDsTEA: high 40+, medium 20-39, low 1-19). Not reworked wholesale.
- Caution text was added (not just removed) for licorice-root, passionflower, ashwagandha and hawthorn, based on NIH pages, because the original text made strong medical claims.
- anji-baicha moved from the high to the medium bucket and rou-gui from high to medium, because they are green tea and oolong. Their quizScore numbers were left alone (caffeine 2 still fits medium).
- Newer emoji (for example the ginger root, bean and jar emoji) may show as empty boxes on older systems; they were left because they are real emoji.
